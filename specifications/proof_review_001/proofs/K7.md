# K7 — Feature coordinates, Gaussian elimination and mode error

## Feature transformation theorem

Let phi(theta) be a p-vector, A an n-by-p coefficient matrix, and M a fixed
invertible p-by-p matrix independent of theta. Define phi_new=M phi and
A_new=A M^-1. Then A_new phi_new=A M^-1 M phi=A phi for every theta, hence
their residuals and squared likelihood norms coincide. Differentiation gives
J_phi_new=M J_phi and A_new J_phi_new=A J_phi, so derivative residuals also agree.
The chronological state generator and endpoint map must either be unchanged or
be transformed with the same exact semantics. This algebra does not certify
conditioning or rounded evaluation. A theta-dependent M would require its
derivatives and usually theta-dependent coefficients; it is outside this claim.

A feature transformation is not a change of physical parameter support. For an
actual C1 diffeomorphism xi=g(theta) between open parameter domains (both g and
its inverse continuously differentiable, with nonsingular Jacobian), density with respect to dxi
is p_theta(g^-1(xi)) |det Dg^-1(xi)|, by change of variables in integrals. Omitting
the Jacobian generally changes probability mass. Prior, transport and support
must follow the parameter map; only proven constant terms cancel on normalization.

## Gaussian elimination theorem

Let x in R^n and y in R^m. For fixed values of any other retained nonlinear
coordinates, take A symmetric, D SPD, B n-by-m, vectors a,b and scalar c. Define

    F(x,y)=x'Ax/2+x'By+y'Dy/2+a'x+b'y+c.

Set d(x)=B'x+b and y*= -D^-1 d(x). Expanding the square gives

    F(x,y) = (y+D^-1 d)'D(y+D^-1 d)/2
              +x'Ax/2+a'x+c-d'D^-1 d/2.

Since D is SPD, the Gaussian integral of the first exponential over R^m is
(2pi)^(m/2) det(D)^(-1/2), obtained by an orthogonal diagonalization and the
one-dimensional Gaussian integral in each coordinate. Therefore

    -log integral_R^m exp(-F(x,y))dy
      = x'(A-BD^-1B')x/2+(a-BD^-1b)'x+c-b'D^-1b/2
        +log(det D)/2-m log(2pi)/2.

This is a pointwise marginal identity; a normalized joint posterior additionally
requires its total integral to be finite. D SPD alone does not guarantee that.
For proper unconstrained Gaussian joint precision, the Schur complement is SPD.
The completed-square derivation supplies the retained curvature and linear term;
using A alone discards their correlation correction.

If y is restricted to measurable S_x, multiply the unconstrained marginal by
the probability that N(y*,D^-1) lies in S_x. The negative log gains minus the
log of that mass (infinite if zero). Thus bounded-support elimination needs that
term. Determinant, square-completion and support-mass terms may depend on retained
nonlinear coordinates and cannot be silently discarded. Unconstrained profiling
over y in R^m retains only the square-completion part and is not marginalization.
Constrained profiling also includes the infimum of the completed nonnegative
square over its support; that penalty need not vanish.

## Discarded-mode theorem

Let the reference whitened residual vector be differentiable r(theta), Jacobian
J, and an approximation be r+e with derivative J+dJ. For objective ||r||^2/2
and its gradient J'r, exact expansion gives

    Delta F = r'e + e'e/2,
    Delta grad F = J'e + dJ'r + dJ'e.

If uniform bounds ||r||<=b, ||e||<=a, ||J||<=j, ||dJ||<=k hold in compatible
Euclidean/operator norms on a declared domain, Cauchy-Schwarz/submultiplicativity
give |Delta F|<=ab+a^2/2 and ||Delta grad F||<=ja+kb+ka. The likelihood-error
oscillation is at most 2ab+a^2 by comparing its values at two points. Prior terms
cancel only if the same prior is retained. Extra query/state transport errors
require their own bounds; these equations concern residual likelihood only.

These constants may grow with prefix length. Small individual coefficients or
bounded storage do not prove a uniform ten-year error. The identities allow
explicit sparse/quantized-mode budgets without restricting continuous batteries
to dictionary nodes. No concrete H7 mode omission or precision reduction is
certified by this lemma.
