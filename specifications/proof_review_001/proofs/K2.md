# K2 — Cumulative optimization error

## Scalar likelihood assumptions and theorem

Let F_0,...,F_m be measurable real negative log densities on the same support D
with the same base measure and finite positive normalizers. Define osc(h) as
sup_(x,y in D)(h(x)-h(y)). Assume osc(F_i-F_(i-1))<=omega_i<infinity.
Telescoping at x and y gives

    (F_m-F_0)(x)-(F_m-F_0)(y)
       = sum_i [(F_i-F_(i-1))(x)-(F_i-F_(i-1))(y)] <= sum_i omega_i.

Taking the supremum proves osc(F_m-F_0)<=Omega=sum omega_i. Each difference
is bounded on a nonempty D because it is finite at one point and has finite
oscillation. Supremum/infimum need not be attained.

For completeness, let d=F_m-F_0 with a<=d<=b and b-a<=Omega, h=exp(-d),
l=exp(-b), u=exp(-a), Z=E_(pi_0)h. Then pi_m/pi_0=h/Z and
TV(pi_m,pi_0)=E|h-Z|/(2Z). The convex chord bound for |h-Z| on [l,u] gives

    TV <= (u-Z)(Z-l)/(Z(u-l)).

If u=l the distributions coincide. Otherwise the right side equals
(u+l-Z-ul/Z)/(u-l). By Z+ul/Z>=2 sqrt(ul), it is at most
(sqrt(u)-sqrt(l))/(sqrt(u)+sqrt(l))=tanh((b-a)/4)<=tanh(Omega/4).
The chord expectation follows by substituting E h=Z into
[(u-h)(Z-l)+(h-l)(u-Z)]/(u-l). Thus the posterior comparison has a full proof,
including normalization, rather than relying on an absolute NLL tolerance.

## Joint precision theorem

At a common prefix and common tangent coordinates, let P_i be SPD and
(1-epsilon_i)P_(i-1)<=P_i<=(1+epsilon_i)P_(i-1), 0<=epsilon_i<1,
where <= is Loewner order. Define a=product(1-epsilon_i)>0 and
b=product(1+epsilon_i). Positive scalar multiplication and transitivity, applied
inductively, give aP_0<=P_m<=bP_0. Congruence by P_0^-1/2 puts its eigenvalues
between a and b, so inversion gives

    b^-1 P_0^-1 <= P_m^-1 <= a^-1 P_0^-1.

For a common query row j, multiplication by j and j' gives the same scalar
variance bounds. If the row changes to j+d, write sigma=||j||_(P_0^-1),
eta=||d||_(P_0^-1). Triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities then bound the
new SD by [max(0,sigma-eta)/sqrt(b), (sigma+eta)/sqrt(a)]. No simultaneous
diagonalization is used. Matrix noncommutation is harmless to this order proof.

Two +10% precision changes can give 1.21 P_0, exceeding the naive 1.20 P_0
upper bound. The products cannot generally be replaced by 1 +/- sum epsilon.
Precisions at different parameterizations/linearization points must first be
related to the declared common tangent model, with extra errors accounted for.

## Point errors and scope

For outputs q_i in one normed space at the same prefix, bounds
||q_i-q_(i-1)||<=d_i imply ||q_m-q_0||<=sum d_i by telescoping and the triangle
inequality. Internal sequential kernels instead require downstream amplification
factors as in a Lipschitz recurrence; they are not automatically common outputs.

These statements justify an immutable-anchor budget across optimization commits.
They do not establish the assumed per-change bounds. The posterior bound is
conditional on data/support; the precision bound concerns a local quadratic.
Neither is a physical coverage proof or a replacement for lifetime risk accounting.
