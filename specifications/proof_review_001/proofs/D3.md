# D3 — Sequential and restart risk accounting

## Risk allocation theorem

Let B_e be the failure event in epoch e and H_e the information sigma-field
available at that epoch's start. Epochs may start at stopping times; an unstarted
epoch has B_e empty and a_e=0. Assume a_e is H_e-measurable, nonnegative,
Pr(B_e | H_e)<=a_e almost surely, and sum_(e>=1) a_e<=alpha_life almost surely.
Then

    Pr(union_e B_e) <= E sum_e 1_Be
                    = sum_e E Pr(B_e|H_e)
                    <= E sum_e a_e <= alpha_life.

The first inequality is the union bound; interchange uses monotone convergence
for nonnegative summands. Epochs need not be independent or disjoint. Predictable
allocation and a valid conditional guarantee are premises, not empirical claims.
For a_e=alpha/[e(e+1)], telescoping 1/[e(e+1)]=1/e-1/(e+1) gives partial sum
alpha m/(m+1)<=alpha. Shrinking allocations may harm availability.

Restarting a per-boot risk a is different: with independent per-boot failures,
the chance of at least one failure after m boots is 1-(1-a)^m. This follows from
multiplying the independent survival probabilities. Without independence that
specific formula need not hold, but resetting a counter still supplies no bound
on the union. SRAM loss therefore needs retained accounting, a qualified bound
on boots with valid allocation, or another lifetime guarantee.

## Supermartingale theorem

Under any fixed admitted parameter theta, suppose M_t(theta) is an adapted,
integrable, nonnegative supermartingale with M_0=1 for a discrete filtration F_t.
Let alpha in (0,1), tau=inf{t: M_t>=1/alpha}. For any finite T, the stopped
process M_(t min tau) is a supermartingale: before tau its conditional next
expectation is at most the current value; after tau it is constant. Iterating
this inequality to T gives E M_(T min tau)<=1. Nonnegativity and
M_tau>=1/alpha on {tau<=T} give Pr(tau<=T)<=alpha. Increasing T and continuity
of probability give Pr(tau<infinity)<=alpha.

Therefore C_t={theta: max_(s<=t) M_s(theta)<1/alpha} simultaneously contains
the true fixed theta with probability at least 1-alpha, assuming the sets and
events are measurable. No union over possible theta is needed: the argument is
under each fixed truth law separately. Computing an outer approximation to C_t
with valid arithmetic is a distinct, potentially expensive implementation problem.

## Causal likelihood construction

Let p_theta,i(y|F_(i-1),action_i) be the correct conditional measurement density
under fixed theta, with action_i known in F_(i-1). Choose a normalized predictive
density q_i measurable from that past before y_i is observed. Assume q_i is
supported where p_theta,i is positive, and ratios are well-defined finite almost
surely under that law. Then

    M_t(theta)=product_(i=1..t) q_i(y_i)/p_theta,i(y_i)

is a nonnegative martingale: conditioning each ratio on the past integrates
q_i to one, so E[M_i|F_(i-1)]=M_(i-1); induction gives integrability and mean one.
Actions using recorded past data are permitted. Choosing q_i after seeing y_i,
using an incorrect ADC/noise likelihood, or ignoring hidden policy information
does not meet these premises. Unmodeled aging invalidates the fixed-theta model.

## Scope

These are mathematical sufficient conditions for time-uniform risk control.
They do not turn posterior TV bounds, local GN SD, repeated tests or simulation
miss rates into a confidence sequence. A prior may be used to construct a proper
predictive q, but H7's output is not itself a proved martingale. No deployed
Mumble process, risk budget, restart mechanism or M23 cost is certified here.
