# K5 — Cost, deadline and memory bounds

## Counted-cost theorem

Partition executed work into disjoint categories: setup, sample, block, refresh,
recovery and other. Suppose their costs have nonnegative uniform upper bounds
C_0,C_s,C_b,C_r,C_f and C_other, and the admitted execution has at most N sample
calls, n_b block calls, n_r refresh calls and n_f recovery calls. Then

    C_total <= C_0+N C_s+n_b C_b+n_r C_r+n_f C_f+C_other.

Proof: sum each operation's cost inequality over its category and then over the
disjoint partition. A bound n_b<=ceil(N/B) requires a separate grouping contract
(B-sized blocks plus at most one final partial block). Retries, replays and repeated
flushes must be counted if that grouping bound does not describe them. Costs must
include target libm, memory behavior, executive, certificates and fault paths;
they cannot be replaced by finite observed maxima without a coverage argument.

If one ready block job has at most B samples, one block operation, one refresh,
r recoveries and other cost C_o, its cost is bounded by
C_job=B C_s+C_b+C_r+r C_f+C_o. This is a count assumption, not a claim about H7.

## Deadline theorem with explicit service premise

A job is released at t with all its inputs ready. At release at most W cycles
of work are ahead of it in a FIFO work-conserving service queue. While that queue
is backlogged, assume the processor supplies at least

    S(t,t+tau) >= s max(0,tau-J)

cycles of useful service for every relevant tau, with s>0 and finite J>=0.
Interference, bounded blocking and clock variation are included in this service
guarantee. Later jobs cannot overtake the job. If

    W+C_job <= s max(0,D-J),

the job completes by t+D. Proof by contradiction: if it were still incomplete,
the queue had remained backlogged until that deadline, so the service bound
would have supplied at least the work ahead plus this job. FIFO would have
completed it, a contradiction. A queue-empty interval means completion already
occurred and needs no lower service bound.

Average utilization rho or nominal rho*F_clk alone does not prove this service
guarantee. A fixed-rate ready-block contract may choose D=B/F_s, but sensor-to-output
latency also includes waiting to collect the block. Arrival jitter, sample deadlines,
nonpreemptible acquisition and bounded backlog W need separate proofs. Deadlines
cannot be inferred from lower average benchmark time.

## Cache cost corollary

Let uncached cost be C, hit cost H, and miss cost M including lookup/fill. With
hit probability h under a declared workload, the expected candidate cost is
hH+(1-h)M by total expectation when H,M are the corresponding exact conditional
mean costs; using conditional upper bounds yields an upper bound instead.
For H<C<M, that expression is below C exactly when h>(M-C)/(M-H).
The worst-case bound remains max(H_max,M_max) under uniform path bounds. A favorable
hit rate does not improve the cold-miss bound. Metadata and cache memory are extra.

## Memory theorem

Suppose a verified concrete layout has disjoint reserved regions with capacities
M_static, M_arena, M_stack, M_other. If (i) all logical allocations fit the arena
layout at every admitted path, with any aliasing justified by lifetime refinement,
(ii) maximum feasible ordinary and interrupt stack nesting fits M_stack, and
(iii) all remaining target memory is accounted in M_other, total SRAM use is at
most their sum. This follows from disjoint physical region sizes and each region's
containment premise. Shared stack/ISR regions are counted once with joint nesting;
separate regions are counted separately. Arithmetic used for addresses/sizes must
be checked for overflow and alignment. Payload liveness alone is not a bound on
allocator fragmentation or an unverified linker layout.

Flash bounds need the final linked image, lookup tables and runtime/software
arithmetic support. Actual M23 costs, scheduling and region premises remain open.
This theorem proves composition of certified bounds, not that any current
implementation meets 10 KiB or a 16 MHz deadline.
