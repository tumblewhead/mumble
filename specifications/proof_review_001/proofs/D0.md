# D0 — Limits of deciding trust from observations

## Assumptions and theorem

Two admitted situations have laws P_g and P_b on the same measurable record
space. An authorization is appropriate in the first and inappropriate in the
second. The complete record includes any information available to the decision
procedure. Let A be its measurable authorization event. Define
TV(P_g,P_b)=sup_B |P_g(B)-P_b(B)|. Then

    P_b(A)+P_g(A^c) >= 1-TV(P_g,P_b).

Proof: P_b(A)+P_g(A^c)=1-[P_g(A)-P_b(A)], and the bracket is bounded above by
the defining supremum. No density, independence, or equal prior odds are required.
For a randomized rule with authorization probability a(y) in [0,1], the same
inequality holds because a(y)=integral_0^1 1{a(y)>t}dt, and integrating the
event-probability difference gives E_g a-E_b a<=TV. Measurability and Tonelli's
theorem justify exchanging the integrals of these bounded nonnegative functions.

If the laws coincide, false authorization plus refusal in the good situation is
at least one. Zero error in both directions is therefore impossible for that
pair. If TV is small, the tradeoff remains substantial. This is a lower bound,
not a proposed optimal decision rule or an estimate of Mumble's actual error rate.

## Interpretation and scope

An unresolved or abstaining output belongs to A^c: it avoids authorization but
can reduce availability. It does not assert the point estimate is physically
wrong. Additional sensors, qualified model restrictions or informative stimuli
can change the two laws and thus the bound; computational precision alone cannot
distinguish identical laws. For adaptive stimuli, P_g and P_b must be the full
records induced by the actual policy, not artificially fixed future actions.

Applying this theorem to particular batteries requires exhibiting the laws and
opposite required decisions. Quiet or confounded data motivate that investigation
but are not, by themselves, proofs of equal observation laws for the full history.
This lemma bounds what any trust classifier can promise; it does not certify
a classifier, sensor model, physical fault detector or population risk.
