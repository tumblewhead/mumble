# D5 — Aging and quiet-period propagation

## Scalar bounded-drift theorem

Let p_k lie in a valid finite interval [l_k,u_k]. If
|p_(k+1)-p_k|<=d_k, d_k>=0, then p_(k+1) lies in
[l_k-d_k,u_k+d_k]. This follows from subtracting/adding d_k to both endpoint
inequalities. Under monotone increase, the tighter interval is [l_k,u_k+d_k];
under monotone decrease it is [l_k-d_k,u_k]. Repeating the argument proves
containment over any finite sequence, with sum d_k when no measurement narrows it.

Intersecting with separately valid physical support preserves truth because it
belongs to both sets. Intersecting with a valid measurement constraint has the
same property. These bounds need not be sharp, and separate scalar boxes do not
preserve joint probability geometry. For coupled quantities use the full reachable
joint set or a proved superset; interval marginals alone are not independent data.

## Monotonicity without a rate is insufficient

Suppose only p(t) in [L,U] and monotone increase, with p(0)=p0<U, are known.
For any Delta>0, the continuous path
p(t)=p0+(U-p0)min(t/Delta,1) is admitted and reaches U by Delta. Therefore no
upper bound below U at that time follows from monotonicity and global support
alone. Decreasing paths have the analogous construction toward L. Requiring
smoothness alone also does not yield a uniform rate. For example, set
b(s)=exp(-1/[s(1-s)]) on (0,1) and zero outside. Its derivatives vanish at the
endpoints because the exponential dominates every reciprocal polynomial.
The normalized cumulative integral of b is a smooth monotone transition from
zero to one; rescaling its argument by t/Delta gives an arbitrarily fast smooth
transition. A qualified derivative bound or other quantitative temporal restriction,
such as a modulus of continuity, can exclude this rescaling; smoothness alone cannot.

Thus monotonicity and support alone cannot justify an upper endpoint below U at
any positive elapsed time for this initial p0<U (or a lower endpoint above L
for decrease from p0>L). Useful narrowness can still follow from the remaining
support: if p0=U-epsilon, [U-epsilon,U] stays valid and can be arbitrarily narrow
without new observations or a rate bound. Away from that case, retaining a tighter
endpoint requires additional valid evolution or measurement constraints. A spline
family or codebook can supply a rate only if its admissible coefficients/times
and approximation remainder prove such a bound for the physical trajectory.
The smoothness or compactness of the stored representation is not that proof.

## Physical interpretation and scope

Monotone aging refers to reference-condition health quantities. Reversible
temperature factors must be transported separately. If p(T)=p_ref k(T), use
the qualified joint uncertainty of p_ref,T and k, rather than assigning temporal
monotonicity to p(T). Unknown thermal laws are model-error premises, not numerical
precision issues. Old measurements constrain old states; a valid evolution model
connects them to current values. Treating every old datum as a stationary current
measurement is not justified by this theorem.

The current stationary H7 reference has no proved aging-rate envelope here.
The proposed 0.5–1.5 health range and manufacturing 3sigma guidance do not supply
one. No arbitrary forgetting, covariance floor or physical aging rate is selected.
This proof specifies honest propagation conditional on a separately qualified
evolution model; useful bounded-memory implementation remains open.
