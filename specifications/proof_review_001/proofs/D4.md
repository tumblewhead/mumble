# D4 — Useful availability and recovery deadlines

## Deterministic enclosure result

For a finite ordered interval [l,u] containing a query range, its midpoint
q=(l+u)/2 has |q-v|<=(u-l)/2 for every v in the interval. Proof: v lies between
the endpoints, whose distances to q are both (u-l)/2. Thus width<=2t is sufficient
for a midpoint to meet absolute tolerance t throughout this enclosure.

For a nonempty bounded **exact attainable** range S with infimum l and supremum u,
every q satisfies sup_(v in S)|q-v|>=max(q-l,u-q)>=(u-l)/2. The first inequality
follows by sequences approaching the infimum/supremum, even when endpoints are
not attained; the second follows since the two terms sum to u-l. Width>2t thus
precludes a uniform tolerance-t point for that exact range. A wide outer enclosure
does not establish this impossibility for the true range.

An arbitrary estimator point q_H7 need not be the midpoint. It must satisfy its
own endpoint-distance test. Merely reducing interval width cannot authorize a
point outside the resulting tolerance band.

## Availability theorem with one probability law

Fix a qualified scenario law P (or a separately qualified conditional law after
a particular recoverable event). Suppose:

1. Under P, event E of simultaneous valid query containment has P(E)>=1-alpha.
2. Under the same P, event A has P(A)>=1-beta. On A, a verified procedure returns
   by time T_build a finite enclosure of width<=2t and completes all validity checks.
3. Once that qualifying result is ready, authorization publishes its midpoint
   within an additional L_pub time units, including scheduling/queue delay.
   Both bounds use the same clock and origin, and T_build+L_pub<=T. The enclosure
   and certificate remain valid for the declared query boundary/horizon at publication;
   any necessary transport/revalidation is included in the bound and event A.

On A it authorizes by T_build+L_pub<=T, giving availability probability at least
1-beta. On
E intersect A the authorized point is also correct by the deterministic result.
The union bound P(E^c union A^c)<=alpha+beta gives correct-and-available probability
at least max(0,1-alpha-beta). Independence is unnecessary.

An implementation that arbitrarily refuses despite satisfying the predicate
does not meet assumption 3. A permanently unresolved implementation cannot claim
this theorem by citing soundness alone. A different reported point needs the
corresponding worst-endpoint predicate and an event A ensuring it passes.

## Conditions requiring their own proofs

N informative samples imply a wall-time deadline only with a qualified arrival
schedule and bounded processing/backlog. An information condition must be an
observable or conservatively validated condition, not a test using unknown truth.
Local curvature or optimizer convergence alone does not prove useful enclosure
width. Recovery excitation must be achievable within power and load constraints.

The probabilities above must concern the same scenario law. An unconditional
alpha cannot be carried unchanged into a data-selected subgroup or post-fault
conditional law without justification. Likewise per-episode beta is not a bound
on all lifetime outages. Fault persistence, absent stimuli or unavailable power
may violate the qualified availability class rather than permit unsafe confidence.

## Scope

This theorem connects valid enclosure production to a useful service guarantee.
It does not prove H7 produces such enclosures or establish N, T, alpha or beta.
Those are concrete implementation/model/target obligations; the theorem prevents
an always-abstaining design from being accepted as useful unattended estimation.
