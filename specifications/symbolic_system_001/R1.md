# R1: RC response, sensitivity and proposed-load voltage

Premises: one Foster branch, fixed R>0 and tau>0, known piecewise-constant current,
actual nonnegative intervals, reference temperature, continuous branch-voltage
state. These statements extend independently to three stationary branches. They
do not select a changing-capacitance state law or treat noisy current as exact.

## Exact response and composition

For interval k put a_k=dt_k/tau and E_k=exp(-a_k). Solving dv/dt=(R I-v)/tau gives

    v_k=E_k*v_(k-1)+R*(1-E_k)*I_k.

Write v_k=F_k*v_0+R*u_k with F_0=1, u_0=0. Then

    F_k=E_k*F_(k-1),
    u_k=E_k*u_(k-1)+(1-E_k)*I_k,
    F_N=exp(-sum dt_k/tau),
    u_N=sum_(j=1..N) I_j*(1-E_j)*product_(l=j+1..N) E_l.

Induction proves both recurrences and the suffix expression. The forced weights
are nonnegative and sum to 1-F_N by telescoping. Thus v_N lies in the convex hull
of v_0 and all R*I_j; this is a physical state bound, not a likelihood/posterior
bound. Interval maps (E,b) compose as (E2*E1,b2+E2*b1), associatively in exact
arithmetic. Swapping whole (current, duration) segments (I1,dt1) and (I2,dt2) changes the
endpoint by R*(1-E1)*(1-E2)*(I2-I1), comparing order (I1 then I2) with (I2 then I1)
when each current retains its own interval duration. Chronology cannot be erased.

At fixed shapes the terminal voltage is additive over branches: -sum_j(F_j*v_j0
+R_j*u_j). It contains no product between distinct pole response functions.
Nevertheless squaring the joint voltage residual produces cross terms between
branches, OCV and hysteresis. Additive response does not permit independent fits
or removal of those likelihood terms.

## Exact log-time-constant sensitivity

Let theta=log(tau), keeping R, v0, inputs and intervals fixed. Since dE/dtheta=aE,

    S_k=d u_k/dtheta=E_k*S_(k-1)+a_k*E_k*(u_(k-1)-I_k), S_0=0,
    dF_N/dtheta=D*exp(-D), D=sum dt/tau.

Differentiate the recurrence to prove S; differentiation of exp(-D) proves the
free-response formula. The second and third free derivatives are respectively
(D^2-D)*exp(-D) and (D^3-3D^2+D)*exp(-D). Their signs change with dose. A universal
bias direction for tau or a universally best rate coordinate does not follow.

The current degree-2 RC field is a full univariate interpolant in each normalized
log(tau) coordinate t, with halfwidth 1 and nodes +a,0,-a, a=sqrt(3)/2. Unlike the
truncated two-dimensional hysteresis field, it omits no term of its quadratic
univariate tensor. In exact arithmetic its response value matches the propagated
nodal response at t=0 for any ordered finite stream. Its derivative there is

    P'(0)=[f(a)-f(-a)]/(2a),

which need not equal f'(0). For f in C^3 on [-a,a], Taylor expansions about zero
with third-derivative remainders give

    |P'(0)-f'(0)| <= a^2/6 * sup_[-a,a] |f'''|.

Thus exact central values do not establish exact rate gradients or precision.
The bound is analytic conditional on a derivative bound; finite probes do not
supply the supremum. Log derivatives for a different halfwidth require its chain.

## Proposed-load consequence

Under a constant future current I for horizon L, starting at branch voltage v,

    v(L)=R*I+(v-R*I)*exp(-L/tau).

Holding this boundary v fixed, partials are

    d v(L)/d v=E,
    d v(L)/d R=I*(1-E),
    d v(L)/d log(tau)=(v-R*I)*(L/tau)*E.

Terminal-voltage RC partials have the opposite signs. If boundary state itself
depends on inferred parameters, include E*d v/dparameter by the chain rule;
dropping that term changes the query. The complete forecast also includes OCV,
Q, R0, hysteresis, temperature and their joint state/parameter uncertainty.

For a continuously differentiable whole-query f and two admissible points connected by a
segment within its smooth domain, exact calculus gives

    f(x+d)-f(x)=integral_0^1 grad f(x+t*d)^T d dt.

A derivative envelope on that segment therefore bounds forecast difference.
The familiar j^T P^-1 j is only a local linear-Gaussian variance when P is the
qualified joint precision; it is not a nonlinear or physical-coverage theorem.
For D=L/tau, the coefficient D*exp(-D) is largest at D=1 (differentiate it).
A query can therefore be relatively insensitive to a poorly known pole far
outside its horizon, subject to the other terms and boundary correlations.
This motivates horizon-aware tests, not authorization to discard a branch.
