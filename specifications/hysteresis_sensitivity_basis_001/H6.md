# H6: conservative uniform hysteresis derivatives and Taylor remainders

Assume the stationary chronological model and independent positive rate coordinates
of H5. Current/sign/intervals do not depend on the query parameters, and each finite
prefix has finite directional throughput. Initial F=1,u=0 and all rate derivatives
are zero. These mathematical bounds do not address floating accumulation or aging.

## Uniform envelopes from the differentiated ODE

In physical time let lambda=k_j |I|/3600 on active direction j and lambda=0 at rest.
The free/forced responses solve F'=-lambda F and u'=lambda(s-u). They obey 0<=F<=1
and |u|<=1 by the positive affine transport weights. Thus |F-0|<=1 and |u-s|<=2.
Every positive-order log-rate derivative of lambda in its active direction equals
lambda; derivatives involving the inactive direction or both directions are zero.

For a multi-index alpha=(a,b), |alpha|>0, differentiation gives a scalar equation
with coefficient -lambda on f_alpha. Its forcing is minus lambda times

    sum_(l=1..alpha_j) binom(alpha_j,l) g_(alpha-l e_j),

where g_beta=f_beta for |beta|>0 and g_0=f-c, with c=0 (free) or s (forced).
The sign is irrelevant for the bound. Sign switches are fixed input boundaries,
not differentiated with respect to a rate. Rest contributes neither forcing nor
decay. The integrating-factor forcing weight has total mass 1-exp(-integral lambda)
<=1, so a uniform bound on the displayed forcing sum bounds f_alpha independently
of duration. Higher derivatives start at zero. Induction on |alpha| proves this.

Define C00=1 and, for |alpha|>0,

    C_alpha=max_(j with alpha_j>0)
        sum_(l=1..alpha_j) binom(alpha_j,l) C_(alpha-l e_j).

Then |F_alpha|<=C_alpha and |u_alpha|<=2 C_alpha for every duration and every pair
of positive stationary rates. The active-direction forcing vanishes if alpha_j=0,
which does not enlarge the maximum. The induction yields

    C10=C01=1; C20=C02=3; C11=1;
    C30=C03=13; C21=C12=3.

These are conservative majorants, not optimal constants or measured accuracy.
For voltage vH=B F+H u with H>=0, set S=|B|+2H. Every third derivative obeys
|partial^alpha vH|<=C_alpha S. This holds uniformly in the rate chart. A uniform
joint amplitude region requires actual bounds on |B| and H. Unbounded amplitudes
do not gain a finite uniform voltage envelope from this proof.

## Local value and rate-gradient error

Let p=|t_plus|, m=|t_minus| and use H5's degree-two Taylor polynomial for vH.
Apply univariate Taylor's integral remainder along the line s -> s*t. Its third
derivative is the multinomial sum of third partials. Therefore

    |vH-P| <= S [13 p^3+9 p^2 m+9 p m^2+13 m^3]/6.

Apply the first-order Taylor remainder to each component of grad(vH), whose
Hessian comprises third derivatives of vH. The component bounds are

    |partial_plus(vH-P)| <= S [13 p^2+6 p m+3 m^2]/2,
    |partial_minus(vH-P)| <= S [3 p^2+6 p m+13 m^2]/2.

For amplitude derivatives, use the value polynomial bound with S=1 for partial_B
(free response), and S=2 for partial_H (forced response). These are partials in
independent amplitudes and log rates; other charts require their full Jacobian.
Combined with P3/M1 they give a route to conservative joint inference bounds,
not an automatic useful precision certificate.

The bounds do not depend on sample count or a minimum physical interval. They
grow with distance from the original centre and may be very loose. Central value,
gradient and Hessian matching does not guarantee useful off-centre accuracy,
physical support of polynomial transport, global posterior fidelity, or uncertainty
coverage. Likelihood errors may accumulate even when response error stays bounded.
No truncation, forgetting, reset of derivative states or chart transition is proved.
