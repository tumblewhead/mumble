## Definition and exact chronological closure

Use stationary reference tau_c>0, R>0, incoming physical branch voltage b,
u=log(tau/tau_c) in the unchanged [-1,1] support, and dimensionless elapsed
time t=age/tau_c. Let rho_j=-1.5+j/4, j=0,...,12, q_j=exp(-rho_j),
lambda_j=q_j/tau_c. These are thirteen distinct fixed poles. The exact query
rate is q(u)=exp(-u), with free response F(t,u)=exp(-t*q(u)). No statement here
covers unknown thermal laws, evolving parameters or aging.

For specificity define the centered cardinal cubic B-spline

    B(x) = (4-6|x|^2+3|x|^3)/6          if |x|<=1,
           (2-|x|)^3/6                  if 1<=|x|<=2,
           0                            otherwise.
    h=1/12; beta_l(u)=B(u/h-l),          l=-13,...,13.

These 27 functions are nonnegative, C2 and sum to one on [-1,1]. To check the
last claim, on each unit cell x=n+r, 0<=r<=1, only the four shifts n-1 through
n+2 contribute; substitution of their cubic expressions gives sum 1. Omitted
shifts at endpoints have value zero. Their first and second derivative sums
are zero by differentiation. Third derivatives are one-sided at knots.

Let A be a fixed real 13-by-27 matrix with each column sum exactly 1 and put
w(u)=A beta(u). Entries of A and w may be signed; beta remains a simplex
vector. Thus sum_j w_j=1. Define parameter-free streamed states

    d xi_j/dage=lambda_j*(I-xi_j),       xi_j(0)=0,
    d phi_j/dage=-lambda_j*phi_j,        phi_j(0)=1,
    vhat=b sum_j w_j phi_j + R sum_j w_j xi_j.

For ordered piecewise constant current and actual interval Delta>=0,

    E_j=exp(-lambda_j*Delta),
    xi_j,new=E_j*xi_j+(1-E_j)*I,
    phi_j,new=E_j*phi_j.

Integration of each scalar linear ODE proves the update, including Delta=0;
induction gives exact chronological closure. Never reset xi at a later rest.
The unknown b stays an amplitude in the readout. Initializing xi with b/R
would make it parameter dependent and is not this construction. States may
be shared across hypotheses only when their fixed poles, input and boundary
origin coincide; arbitrary incoming compressed histories need their own valid
boundary representation and cannot be replaced by a fresh scalar reset.

Write Fhat=sum w_j exp(-q_j t), khat=sum w_j q_j exp(-q_j t). Integration gives
uhat=khat*I in dimensionless time, and Fhat(0,u)=1. For constant current from
the boundary xi_j=I(1-phi_j), hence uhat=I(1-Fhat), exactly. Thus b=RI implies
vhat=RI. The same weights on free and forced responses are essential.

For fixed states, vhat_u=b w' phi+R w' xi, vhat_uu=b w'' phi+R w'' xi,
vhat_b=w phi and vhat_R=w xi. Mixed derivatives follow by differentiating
these formulas; physical coordinate chains must also be retained. In
particular, the b terms in shape sensitivities cannot be omitted. One joint
likelihood must retain all branch, amplitude, OCV and hysteresis cross terms;
derivative features are not new observations. Streaming state count and lifted
likelihood width are different quantities: 13 poles and 27 spline coefficients
do not establish the existing W<=34 budget or complete M23 memory feasibility.

## Conditional physical constraints, with proofs

For column l define f_l(t)=sum_j A_jl exp(-q_j t),
k_l(t)=sum_j A_jl q_j exp(-q_j t), and
G_l(s)=sum_j A_jl q_j/(s+q_j). Impose, for every column,

    k_l(t)>=0 for all t>=0,                            (M)
    Re G_l(i omega)>=0 for all real omega.             (E)

These are sufficient conditions, not necessary conditions for the restricted
spline image w([-1,1]). Signed A is permitted. Since f_l(0)=1,
f_l(infinity)=0 and f_l'=-k_l, (M) gives 0<=f_l<=1 and integral k_l=1.
Convex mixing with beta proves the same for Fhat and khat at each fixed u.
Consequently |uhat|<=Imax for |I|<=Imax from zero forced state, by the integral
triangle inequality. Monotonicity alone does not prove (E).

Here is a direct zero-state energy proof for (E). For real compactly supported
square-integrable I, extend I by zero outside its time interval. Each exponential
kernel is integrable, so convolution and the Fourier inner-product identity give

    integral I(t)*R*uhat(t) dt
      = R/(2*pi) integral Re G(i omega)*|Itilde(omega)|^2 d omega >=0,
    G=sum_l beta_l(u) G_l.

The identity follows first for smooth compact inputs by inserting the Fourier
representations and integrating, then for L2 inputs by density and boundedness
of convolution by an L1 kernel. Taking I zero after any selected finite horizon
proves the finite-horizon supply inequality from zero initial state. Positivity
is preserved by the simplex sum, which proves the assertion without a KYP
existence theorem. For nonzero stored initial energy a separate storage
certificate suffices: with L=diag(q_j), c=w, suppose symmetric P>=0 satisfies
P*q=c and PL+LP>=0, where q=(q_j). For x'=-Lx+q I and output c^T x,
S=R*x^T P*x/2 gives S'<=R*I*c^T x by direct differentiation. This is only a
conditional storage construction; existence of P is not asserted here.

All energy claims fix u during the trajectory. Switching query parameters or
allowing an evolving u introduces extra terms (including P'(u)u' if applicable).
Per-query passivity is not passivity of an evolving-parameter physical system,
nor whole-cell electrochemical passivity.

## Exact derivative formulas for error certification

Use t for dimensionless elapsed time and u for log tau; z=t*q(u). Define

    P_0(z)=1; P_(k+1)(z)=z*(P_k(z)-P_k'(z)).
    partial_u^k F=P_k(z)*exp(-z).
    Q_0k=P_k; Q_(a+1,k)=Q_ak'-Q_ak.
    partial_t^a partial_u^k F=q(u)^a Q_ak(z)*exp(-z).

The first recursion uses partial_u z=-z; the second differentiates in t,
where q is constant. Induction proves both. In particular Q_(a+1,k) is NOT
z*(Q_ak-Q_ak'); that latter operation is a log-rate derivative, not a time
derivative. For the physical impulse kernel k=q exp(-z), set

    R_0(z)=z; R_(m+1)(z)=z*(R_m(z)-R_m'(z)),
    partial_u^m k=R_m(z)*exp(-z)/t,                    t>0.

Differentiation at fixed t proves this recurrence. Every R_m is divisible by
z, so its continuous t=0 limit exists and equals (-1)^m q. The equivalent
form q*(R_m(z)/z)*exp(-z) uses that continuous extension. The bank derivatives
are simply sum_j w_j^(k)(u)(-q_j)^a exp(-q_j t); kernel derivatives replace
the summand by w_j^(k) q_j exp(-q_j t). The C2 splines support global k<=2;
higher derivatives require knot partitioning and one-sided statements.

## Finite-cell and tail certificate recipe, including uncertainty

Every following bound is conditional on validated interval inputs and outward
arithmetic. Decimal samples without rounding bounds are not interval evidence.
Let g(t,u) be a defect derivative, a column kernel, or another scalar to be
bounded. On a rectangle centered at (t0,u0) with halfwidths (dt,du), let its
center enclosure be [g0-,g0+] and verified derivative suprema be Lt,Lu. The
fundamental theorem of calculus along an axis path proves

    g in [g0- - Lt*dt - Lu*du, g0+ + Lt*dt + Lu*du].

An enclosure of the full symbolic expression on the cell is also sufficient.
Split at spline knots. For derivative orders with a bounded piecewise next
derivative, continuity and integrating each knot interval give the same
Lipschitz bound. Refine until sign/error margins close; a failure to close is
unresolved, not a physical exclusion. This formula specifies the grid error;
no numeric grid error or 13-by-27 success count is independently asserted.

For coefficient intervals A_jl in [a_jl-,a_jl+], bound every linear combination
by interval arithmetic (or midpoint plus sum radius*absolute basis). Enforce
column sums exactly via a dependent coefficient, or retain their uncertain
normalization explicitly; intervals merely containing sum 1 do not prove it.
Rate and spline arithmetic uncertainty must likewise be enclosed. An interval
family certificate proves all matrices in the admitted family; it cannot turn
an unspecified or rounded A into a known exact candidate.

For usable infinite tails define

    H_n(T,r)=integral_T^infinity t^n exp(-r*t) dt
      = exp(-r*T) sum_(m=0..n) n!/(n-m)! * T^(n-m)/r^(m+1).

Integration by parts, with H_0=exp(-rT)/r, proves the formula by induction.
For a polynomial p(t)=sum c_n t^n, triangle inequality gives
integral_T^infinity |p(t)| exp(-r t) dt <=sum |c_n| H_n(T,r).
For a supremum tail, each t^n exp(-r t) reaches its maximum on [T,infinity)
at t=max(T,n/r) (n=0 at T), as direct differentiation proves. Coefficient
interval absolute maxima and positive rate lower bounds make these bounds
robust to uncertainty. Combining them with the preceding derivative recursions
bounds free-response suprema and kernel-derivative L1 tails uniformly in u.

For example put C_jk=sup_u |w_j^(k)(u)|. Then the bank's kth shape derivative
tail has supremum <=sum C_jk exp(-q_j T) and its kernel tail L1 bound is the
same sum. The exact F derivative has the polynomial P_k(t q) and the exact
kernel derivative has q*(R_k(tq)/(tq)); expand them into powers of t, bound
coefficients on q in [exp(-1),exp(1)], and use r=exp(-1) in the formulas above.
Add exact and bank bounds to obtain defect tails. This is conservative but
fully specified; no asymptotic smallness claim supplies a numeric tolerance.

To prove (M) on the tail, order rates increasingly and, for each column, select
its slowest nonzero coefficient at index s. If its validated lower bound is
a_sl->0, a sufficient robust condition is

    a_sl- q_s >= sum_(j>s) max(0,-a_jl-) q_j
                                      exp(-(q_j-q_s)T).

Factoring exp(-q_s t) proves nonnegativity for t>=T; positive later terms can
be discarded and every negative ratio decreases with t. Earlier coefficients
must be exactly zero. Rate uncertainty requires certified ordering and lower
bounds on gaps and corresponding interval products. If the slowest nonzero
coefficient is negative, the tail eventually fails; if its interval crosses
zero this argument is unresolved. Zero columns are incompatible with sum 1.

For (E), set x=omega^2. Its positive denominator D(x)=product_j(q_j^2+x)
leaves the exact polynomial

    N_l(x)=sum_j A_jl q_j^2 product_(i!=j)(q_i^2+x).

Thus N_l>=0 on x>=0 suffices. On each finite interval convert this polynomial
exactly to Bernstein form; nonnegative coefficient lower bounds imply positivity
because Bernstein functions are nonnegative and sum to one. On the infinite
tail x>=X>0 put y=1/x and certify y^12 N_l(1/y) on [0,1/X] in the same way.
Degree is at most 12, so this is an ordinary polynomial including y=0.
Subdivide if needed. This gives a finite sufficient certificate with explicit
interval arithmetic; it does not assert that every nonnegative polynomial has
a strictly positive coefficient certificate after a finite subdivision.

If E_k=sup_(t,u)|partial_u^k(Fhat-F)| and
K_k=sup_u integral_0^infinity |partial_u^k(khat-k)| dt are certified by finite
cells plus these tails, convolution and the triangle inequality prove

    |partial_u^k(vhat-v)| <= |b| E_k+R Imax K_k,       k=0,1,2.

For K_k, integrate each finite time cell's uniform absolute upper bound times
its length and add the tail; maximize over the covering u cells. The stated
fixed-amplitude formula does not omit b; if b or R depends on the queried
coordinate use the full product rule. Physical-time kernels include 1/tau_c;
the time change cancels this factor in the L1 norm. These are response bounds,
not fitted bias, validated uncertainty coverage or a lifetime likelihood bound.

## Obstructions, shift reuse and limits

The positive-weight center obstruction from P1 has a short independent proof.
If nonnegative C1 weights sum to one and reproduce exp(-q_c t) exactly at an
interior query center for every t, derivatives at t=0 give mean q_c and second
moment q_c^2. Zero variance forces all weight onto the one pole q_c (or makes
equality impossible if it is absent). Every inactive weight has a local minimum
zero, so its query derivative is zero. Normalization makes the remaining
derivative zero too. The bank sensitivity is therefore zero, contradicting
t*q_c exp(-t*q_c)>0 for t>0. Signed weights avoid this specific proof, but
must separately satisfy the conditional physical constraints above.

Linear interpolation of a C2 function between neighboring log-rate nodes gives
an O(h^2) upper error by Taylor's integral remainder (bounded second derivative
times at most h^2/8). It supplies no universal O(h^2) lower error gap. A query
may approach a node arbitrarily closely or coincide with it; moments alone do
not establish a response-norm lower bound. Even a point kept a specified
fraction of a cell away from nodes requires a separate quantitative theorem
for the particular admissible weights and norm. No uniform such gap, or
exclusion of all structural cones, is proved here. P4's repeated-pole floor
does not apply automatically to this distinct-pole signed family.

If branch log centers differ by integer multiples k_r of the pole spacing,
their pole sets are index intervals {k_r,...,k_r+N-1}. The containing bank has
N+max k_r-min k_r consecutive indices. This equals the union count only when
these intervals overlap or touch so no indices are missing; otherwise the true
union is smaller. Proof: count integers from the least starting index through
the largest ending index, then remove any gaps. Reuse never licenses truncating
end poles, narrowing physical support, moving a center after evidence was
recorded, or discarding each branch's boundary amplitude and cross terms.
Nonintegral center shifts need additional poles or a separately bounded
approximation, not an integer-shift identity.
