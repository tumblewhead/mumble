## C1.1 Fixed features give an exact surrogate likelihood

For a fixed prefix and declared surrogate suppose each normalized observation is

    r_i(theta) = z_i^T a(theta) - y_i.

The finite feature z_i and normalized datum y_i are fixed when querying theta.
They may depend on known current, actual time intervals and frozen causal anchors
computed from earlier data. Freezing an anchor is essential: a changed candidate
theta cannot regenerate old z_i from an unavailable stream. Data-adaptive anchor
selection defines a conditional surrogate algorithm; this algebra alone does not
prove that its observation model equals the original physical generative model.
Noise weights here are known and candidate-independent. Parameter-dependent
noise needs its own log-normalizer and weighted-statistic closure.

Let d_i=[z_i;-y_i], c(theta)=[a(theta);1], W=dim(c), and

    G_N = sum_(i=1)^N d_i d_i^T,       U_N^T U_N = G_N.
    ell_N(theta) = 1/2 c(theta)^T G_N c(theta)
                = 1/2 ||U_N c(theta)||^2.

Expanding each square proves the equality in exact arithmetic; adding one record
adds d_i d_i^T, and QR insertion preserves the same Gram matrix. Rank deficiency
does not invalidate the identity and must not be repaired with invented evidence.
All feature cross products are retained, including between SOC/Q, three RC
branches, hysteresis and incoming-state coefficients. The original full joint
prior is added ONCE outside this history likelihood. Evidence from a prefix must
not also enter through a posterior already derived from that prefix. Priors and
state-boundary messages require explicit, nonoverlapping source ownership.

The exact claim concerns this surrogate, irrespective of whether a(theta) is
nonlinear. It does not establish exact physical likelihood, calibrated coverage,
bounded numerical error, or bounded storage for the coefficient generator itself.
Transport of all uncertain states and its sensitivities remains a separate joint
obligation; a voltage-likelihood factor alone is not a boundary-state posterior.

## C1.2 Exact algebraic compression and the scaling correction

Suppose an identity a(theta)=a0+T phi(theta) holds for EVERY theta in the original
admitted physical domain, with fixed T and a0. Then

    z_i^T a(theta)-y_i = (T^T z_i)^T phi(theta) - (y_i-z_i^T a0).

The transformed augmented rows therefore preserve the complete surrogate
likelihood exactly. Coefficients shared algebraically by different feature
blocks can be merged by summing their features. The proof is substitution; it
requires a global coefficient identity, not a good fit at sampled parameters.

In the proposed scaled construction D=S(a-a0)=U phi, for invertible fixed S,

    a-a0 = S^-1 U phi = T phi;         transformed feature = T^T z.

Using S U instead of S^-1 U is generally wrong. For example S=2 and U=1 imply
a-a0=phi/2, so the feature is z/2, not 2z. This correction is necessary even when
S is diagonal. A sampled SVD threshold of 1e-12 is a numerical rank heuristic;
it neither proves a global identity nor supplies an error bound between samples.
An analytic identity or a certified uniform bound on the omitted coefficients is
required. If S is singular this inverse construction is not justified.

For approximate a=a0+T phi+e_a, the residual defect is z_i^T e_a. A small
coefficient norm alone says little without feature magnitudes and their cumulative
cross products. The exact old residual and the defect both enter the likelihood
bound below. Updating a learned T later requires a lossless transform from the
old representation or access to its original rows; otherwise that is another
approximation with another error account.

## C1.3 Cold-start and hysteresis are still uncertain physical states

A stationary branch has v_j(t)=b_j exp(-t/tau_j)+R_j(k_j*I)(t).
The three b_j are uncertain incoming states, jointly coupled to the other
parameters. A finite response bank can express an approximation

    exp(-t/tau) approximately Phi_V(t)^T w(tau),

giving free-response coefficient b_j w(tau_j). This is not a proof that cold
start is represented exactly. Needed bounds range over the full time horizon,
tau domain and incoming-state support, and include derivatives and transport.
If |b_j|<=B_j, a uniform kernel defect eta_j gives voltage defect at most
B_j eta_j; an unbounded b_j prior needs a stated tail-probability/tail-moment
argument instead of this supremum. Any restricted-domain posterior guarantee
also needs control of probability outside that domain.

Positive-rate exponentials never become exactly zero at finite time. Small
current free response does not erase the old cross moments with observations.
Dropping b_j or its old likelihood requires a certified inference approximation,
including correlations and tail uncertainty, not a decay threshold alone.

For illustration only, known hysteresis rate and known Q would make its driven
trajectory a known filter, with unknown amplitude/initial state multiplying known
features. That reduced problem is not ours. Here gamma_plus, gamma_minus, Q,
H and incoming hysteresis state remain uncertain, with chronological sign changes
and accumulated throughput. Their coefficient map or approximation must preserve
those dependencies and joint cross terms. One must not freeze a rate to obtain
an advertised feature count for the full 16-quantity problem. The same caution
applies to parameter-dependent temperature response and physical aging; neither
extension follows from a stationary fixed-temperature derivation.

## C1.4 Nonlinear static OCV: a sufficient construction, not a universal theorem

For SOC s_i=x-c q_i with known cumulative charge q_i and c=1/Q, a polynomial
OCV f(s)=sum_(k=0)^d alpha_k s^k gives

    f(x-c q_i) = sum_(j=0)^d q_i^j
                 sum_(k=j)^d alpha_k binom(k,j) x^(k-j) (-c)^j.

Thus d+1 known charge features give exact voltage closure for this static
polynomial OCV model, before adding the other voltage components. All charge
moments needed by squared residuals are then retained by the full Gram factor.
This sufficiency proof is the binomial theorem. A general calibrated nonlinear
OCV table does not acquire this closure merely by being smooth.

We do NOT claim that finite statistics exist if and only if f is polynomial for
arbitrary statistical encodings, domains or parameter restrictions. For example
with known c, f(s)=exp(s) separates into exp(x)exp(-c q), a single feature even
though f is not polynomial. With unknown c in an open interval and distinct
positive q_i, those exponentials instead have arbitrarily large linear rank.
The Vandermonde derivative argument in B1 proves the latter fixed-linear-feature
obstruction. It extends immediately to OCV exp(x-c q) by fixing x and varying c.
Neither this example nor polynomial sufficiency proves a general necessity
theorem for all smooth OCV curves or nonlinear sufficient-statistic encodings.
Approximate polynomial/table closure must carry interpolation, derivative and
domain/tail errors; no general exact finite-OCV-statistic claim is accepted here.

## C1.5 What a likelihood error certificate would buy

Let the reference residual vector be r, the surrogate be r+e, with the same prior
and observation normalizer. Exactly,

    Delta ell = r^T e + 1/2 ||e||^2,
    |Delta ell| <= ||r|| ||e|| + 1/2 ||e||^2.

Assume a uniform bound |Delta ell(theta)|<=delta on the ENTIRE common support,
finite nonzero normalizing constants, and the same prior measure. Then
exp(-delta)<=Lhat/L<=exp(delta). Integrating against the prior gives the same
bounds for Zhat/Z, and division proves

    exp(-2delta) <= d posterior_hat / d posterior <= exp(2delta).

Consequently every measurable event has probabilities bounded by the same
multipliers (with the usual cap at one). This is a comparison to the reference
posterior, not a truth-coverage theorem. If the bound only holds in a local box,
the conclusion holds for the two posteriors conditioned on that box; their
unconditional masses/tails remain unbounded by this argument.

For disjoint evidence batches with uniform defects delta_k, the triangle
inequality gives delta_total<=sum_k delta_k. This also applies to successive
replacement operations if each certificate compares the new potential to the
previous one on the same support and each defect is charged once. Fixed per-row
voltage error is not a bounded-lifetime likelihood error: for N rows with
|r_i|<=R and |e_i|<=eta it only gives delta<=N(R eta+eta^2/2).
The score, exact Hessian and transported-query objectives of P2 need additional
derivative bounds; a likelihood sup norm alone is not a local-curvature bound.

Thus approximate compression is defensible with an explicit cumulative budget
and a justified recovery/refusal rule. No all-prefix budget, physical residual
envelope, posterior tail bound or target allocation is supplied by this packet.

## C1.6 Why a small local Laplace trigger does not certify old evidence

Consider old negative log likelihood

    V(theta)=theta^2/2+epsilon theta^4/4, epsilon>0,

and its archived quadratic V0(theta)=theta^2/2. On |theta|<=r the Hessian
variation relative to V''(0)=1 is at most 3 epsilon r^2, which can be below 0.05.
Now add new negative log likelihood K(theta-M)^2/2. The quadratic archive's
minimizer is theta0=KM/(1+K); the retained reference minimizer uniquely solves

    (1+K) theta + epsilon theta^3 = KM.

Strictly increasing left side proves uniqueness. For fixed K>0 and large M,
theta grows on the order of M^(1/3), whereas theta0 grows linearly with M.
The fit discrepancy is unbounded although the original local trigger can be
arbitrarily small. At the shifted solution the archived old curvature also
misses 3 epsilon theta^2. This example needs no simulator or numerical fit.

On a bounded physical domain the same example demonstrates loss outside the
certified local box whenever that domain contains such points; it does not
assert an unbounded battery parameter error. A criterion named omega<0.05
based on local curvature alone cannot control global archived likelihood error.
After deleting the quartic term, later likelihood information cannot recover it
from the saved quadratic. Keeping raw recent data addresses new terms only.
A valid Laplace collapse needs uniform remainder/tail certificates covering all
future queried support, a bounded accumulated replacement error, or a recoverable
representation. None is established by local Hessian stability.

## C1.7 Costs and status

A packed W by W upper root contains W(W+1)/2 scalars, including the response
normalizer coordinate in W. Binary64 payload is 4W(W+1) bytes: W=34 costs
4760 B. Binary32 encoding halves those bytes, but QR rounding, coefficient
evaluation and repeated update error require a cumulative certificate. A stable
individual QR step does not by itself prove bounded all-prefix error, and float32
storage is not a claim about float32 arithmetic or Cortex-M23 execution speed.

For K Gaussian/Laplace components in p physical coordinates, covariance/precision
roots alone cost K p(p+1)/2 scalars. Means add Kp, weights at least K-1, and
normalizers/support metadata and transport are additional. For p=16 one binary64
root is 1088 B and its mean is 128 B, already 1216 B before other fields. This
count grants no mixture approximation error certificate or bounded component
growth. Replacing a joint root by separate blocks would delete cross information.

The comparison limits remain W<=34 and response state<=312 B. Persistent tables,
full prior root, current/trial parameter vectors, state sensitivities, QR rows,
solver/query scratch, transaction handling, stack, queues and fault recovery must
all enter a complete lifecycle ledger. P3's named 8568 B subtotal (9528 B with
general prior) has explicit exclusions and is not measured complete-C SRAM.
No algebraic rank saving here establishes that complete target implementation.

Conditional conclusions: exact Gram closure and globally justified coefficient
merges are valid for a declared fixed-feature surrogate; the scaled transform
requires S^-1; polynomial OCV sufficiency and the stated linear-feature
exponential obstruction are narrow results; uniform likelihood error would yield
the posterior-ratio bound above. OPEN: useful full-model response/derivative and
transport bounds, cold-start/tail control, cumulative arithmetic and replacement
error, sustained useful joint inference, and complete target cost. Local SVD and
Laplace heuristics alone discharge none of these obligations.
