# H2: repetitive-use geometry and why rate-polynomial behavior changes

Assume H1's stationary exact-input model. An identical repeated current word has
a fixed affine map h_next=E*h+b with0<=E<1. Iterating a geometric series gives

    h_n = h_star + E^n*(h0-h_star),  h_star=b/(1-E).

The limit is in[-1,1] by H1. Rest-only E=1,b=0 instead preserves h0 and has no
unique attracting point. The word can contain arbitrary sign segments; knowing
its endpoint map is not the same as preserving all within-word likelihoods.

## Two-direction cycle

For positive then negative throughput Uplus,Uminus, set
xplus=kplus*Uplus, xminus=kminus*Uminus, a=exp(-xplus), b=exp(-xminus).
At cycle endpoints after the negative segment, the affine map is

    h_next = a*b*h + b*(1-a)-(1-b).

For D=xplus+xminus>0, its fixed endpoint h_minus_star and the intervening positive
endpoint h_plus_star have midpoint c and half-span w:

    delta=xplus-xminus,
    c=(b-a)/(1-a*b) = sinh(delta/2)/sinh(D/2),
    w=(1-a)*(1-b)/(1-a*b)
      = [cosh(D/2)-cosh(delta/2)]/sinh(D/2),
    h_plus_star=c+w, h_minus_star=c-w.

Substitute the affine fixed point to obtain both endpoints, then add/subtract;
the hyperbolic forms follow by factoring exp(-D/2). D controls memory of the
initial state (E=exp(-D)); delta controls directional imbalance together with D.
These are stimulus-dependent coordinates, not universal replacements for battery
parameters. On a balanced-dose cycle xplus=xminus=x, c=0,w=tanh(x/2).
For small D at fixed delta/D, c tends to delta/D and
w tends to (D^2-delta^2)/(4D). The limit is along that fixed ratio, not a unique
fixed point at D=0. c,w are hysteresis-state geometry, not posterior uncertainty.

The attracting midpoint can create an OCV-like voltage offset H*c. Approximation
of this midpoint or its chronological transients can project onto SOC and Q(H3).
This explains a possible mechanism, not proof that every studied load is this
two-segment periodic cycle. The multiscale fixture is a repeated multisegment
word; the general affine iteration applies at its matching phases. Embedded
stimuli need not repeat and have no single fixed c,w.

## Rate curvature is controlled by exposure

For one suffix kernel E=exp(-kplus*Aplus-kminus*Aminus), write d_s=k_s*A_s.
In log-rate coordinates theta_s=log(k_s), direct differentiation gives

    dE/dtheta_s = -d_s E,
    d2E/dtheta_s^2 = (d_s^2-d_s) E,
    d2E/(dtheta_plus dtheta_minus) = d_plus*d_minus*E.

The scalar first-derivative magnitude d*exp(-d) peaks at d=1; with the other dose
held fixed it is multiplied by that other exponential. Mixed/higher derivatives
cannot generally be replaced by independent one-dimensional fits. The free
response kernel is separable exactly, yet a low-total-degree polynomial need
not preserve its product form. The forced response contains many such kernels
with different exposures and signed weights. No single exposure inferred from
total elapsed time captures them all.

For a *fixed finite* current word scaled by epsilon, its response is analytic
in epsilon. At order n its coefficient is a polynomial of total degree<=n in
kplus,kminus, by expansion of the finite products/sums of exponentials in H1.
A fixed bounded projector reproducing rate polynomials through total degree d
therefore has O(epsilon^(d+1)) error on a compact rate domain as epsilon goes to0.
A polynomial in log rates generically fails to reproduce even the linear-rate
coefficient, leaving O(epsilon) error unless cancellation removes it. This is the
earlier weak-exposure result restated in current units; it is not uniform in
word length, accumulated dose, all derivatives or arbitrary parameter support.

For an individual kernel, a local Taylor expansion in its scalar dose about D0
has the exact-real remainder bound

    |R_d| <= exp(-min(D,D0))*|D-D0|^(d+1)/(d+1)!,  D,D0>=0.

Taylor's theorem applies because the(d+1)st derivative of exp(-D) has magnitude
exp(-D), whose maximum on the intervening interval is exp(-min(D,D0)). Summing
such bounds with absolute signed coefficients bounds a finite switch expansion,
but may be loose and grow with reversal count. It is not a bound for the current
Chebyshev interpolation/projection, nor for a normalized posterior or fitted Q.

This resolves the apparent conflict between the earlier short weak-exposure
success of physical-rate polynomials and their recent longer-prefix regression:
the former result is asymptotic for a fixed word with small dose. It provides no
guaranteed ranking at O(1) or larger dose, with different domains/degrees. The
actual finite fixtures and their exposures must be examined; this statement
alone does not assign a quantitative share of observed error to a particular term.

## Endpoint simplification is not likelihood closure

Even for one observation at a fixed phase of an exactly repeated word, the
transient is proportional to E^n. With other voltage terms known/subtracted,
the least-squares cross term contains the function

    P_N(E) = sum_(n=0..N-1) y_n E^n.

For arbitrary measured y_n these span an N-dimensional polynomial space on any
open E interval. Linear independence follows because a polynomial identically
zero on an interval has every coefficient zero. Therefore no fixed-dimensional
linear function basis can represent all such cross terms exactly as N increases.
A concrete restricted example is a one-sign repeated segment with fixed initial
h0 and H, for which h_n=s+(h0-s)E^n (nonzero transient), so this cross term already
occurs without a more complicated cycle. Index shifting only multiplies P by E.

This does not rule out bounded approximate compression, special observation
sequences, or nonlinear encodings with separately qualified resources/error. It
rules out inferring exact fixed-basis likelihood closure from the two-number
endpoint map. Physical decay of old initial-state influence is not deletion of
old statistical evidence. No ten-year or total SRAM theorem is claimed.
