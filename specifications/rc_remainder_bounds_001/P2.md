# P2: uniform stationary Foster derivative envelopes

## Premises and convolution

Fix a branch's R>0, tau>0 and initial voltage b. Inputs are measurable,
|I(s)|<=Imax, on any finite [0,T]; piecewise-constant sampled inputs with actual
nonnegative intervals are included. Hold b, R, T and I fixed when differentiating
theta=log(tau). Zero-duration observations add no physical evolution. This is the
stationary reference-temperature, continuous-voltage law of R1, in exact arithmetic.
It is not a law for changing C, unknown thermal calibration, or aging.

Solving dv/ds=(R I-v)/tau gives

    v(T;tau)=b F(T;tau)+R u(T;tau),
    F=exp(-T/tau),
    u=integral_0^T k_tau(T-s) I(s) ds,
    k_tau(w)=tau^-1 exp(-w/tau).

For x=w/tau and D=T/tau define polynomials with ascending coefficients:

    p0=1, p_(n+1)=(x-1)p_n-x p_n',
    q0=1, q_(n+1)=x(q_n-q_n').

The product and chain rules give d_theta^n k=k*p_n(x) and
d_theta^n F=exp(-D)*q_n(D). For tau in any compact positive interval,
the differentiated integrands on finite [0,T] are dominated by an integrable
constant times |I|. Differentiation under the integral is therefore valid.
In particular:

    p3=x^3-6x^2+7x-1,       q3=x^3-3x^2+x,
    p4=x^4-10x^3+25x^2-15x+1,
    q4=x^4-6x^3+7x^2-x.

For n>=1, p_n=q_n-q_n'. This follows from
integral_0^T k ds=1-F, or from the polynomial recurrences by induction.
Both q_n(0)=0 and exp(-x)q_n(x)->0 as x->infinity.

## Constants and proof of the duration-independent bounds

Let

    A_n=sup_(x>=0) |exp(-x)q_n(x)|,
    B_n=integral_0^infinity exp(-x)|p_n(x)| dx.

Then for every T>=0 and every tau>0,

    |d_theta^n F|<=A_n,
    |d_theta^n u|<=Imax B_n,
    |d_theta^n v|<=|b| A_n+R Imax B_n.

The first bound is its definition. For the second use the convolution derivative,
take absolute values and substitute x=(T-s)/tau; extend the resulting nonnegative
integral from [0,D] to [0,infinity). The third is the triangle inequality. These
bounds contain no sample count, duration or minimum time step. Chronology is still
required to evaluate the response; the bound does not erase it. The forced B_n
bound is sharp as a supremum over bounded measurable currents and all durations:
at a fixed endpoint choose I(T-tau*x)=Imax sign(p_n(x)) over [0,D], then let D grow.
It need not be sharp for a particular current sequence or combined free/forced term.

The following rational upper constants suffice:

| n | A_n upper | B_n upper |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3 | 0.42962 | 1.78302 |
| 4 | 1.11875 | 4.51031 |

These decimals denote exact rational numbers. Their certificate uses the following
integer-numerator root brackets with denominator 10^10:

    p3: [1657568156,1657568157], [13433795689,13433795690],
        [44908636153,44908636154]
    p4: [760038343,760038344], [7621690715,7621690716],
        [26516929955,26516929956], [65101340985,65101340986].

Exact rational endpoint evaluations have opposite signs on each disjoint positive
bracket. There are n brackets for a degree-n polynomial, hence these exhaust its
roots, all simple. Since (exp(-x)q_n)'=-exp(-x)p_n, its only interior extrema are
these roots. Its endpoints are both zero. If z_i=exp(-r_i)q_n(r_i), then

    A_n=max_i |z_i|,
    B_n=sum_(i=0..n) |z_(i+1)-z_i|, with z_0=z_(n+1)=0.

The second identity integrates the absolute derivative over each monotone interval.
Rational interval Horner arithmetic encloses q_n(r_i). For rational x>=0 and
m=48 with x<m+2, let S=sum_(k=0..m) x^k/k!, h=x^(m+1)/(m+1)!. Positivity and a
geometric bound on the remaining term ratios give

    S <= exp(x) <= S+h/(1-x/(m+2)),
    1/(S+h/(1-x/(m+2))) <= exp(-x) <= 1/S.

Use monotonicity of exp(-x) at each bracket endpoint and multiply intervals.
The checker compares the resulting A_n/B_n upper fractions to the stated rational
constants exactly, without floating root solving, quadrature or rounded comparison.
Together with the preceding analysis, this is a finite certificate of the bounds.

## Consequence for P1 and limits

With fixed centre t=log(tau/tau_c), d/dt=d/dtheta. For each admitted amplitude pair
define M3=0.42962*|b|+1.78302*R*Imax and
M4=1.11875*|b|+4.51031*R*Imax. They hold uniformly over every log-tau interval.
P1's Taylor value/slope remainders are M3*|t|^3/6 and M3*t^2/2. Its symmetric
Hermite value bound on [-1,1] is M4/96; its stated Peano-kernel slope bound also
applies with this M4. No new uniform Peano-kernel norm is asserted here.
For uniform amplitude support replace |b|,R by valid upper bounds on that support.
Unbounded amplitude support does not acquire a uniform finite M_n by this proof.

This bounds the mathematical response represented from one fixed centre, not
floating accumulation in its stored sensitivities. It does not permit resetting
derivative states, dropping data, silently recentering, or shrinking posterior
support. A known temperature time change might extend the result, but is not
proved here; uncertainty in paths and state conventions requires separate work.
