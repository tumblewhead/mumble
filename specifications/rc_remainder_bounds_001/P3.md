# P3: response bounds, admitted queries and joint inference consequences

## P3a: conditional RC error budgets

Assume P2 and P1; all RC responses are Taylor2 about their original fixed centres.
Use independent coordinates (b_j,R_j,t_j), j=1..3, with t_j=log(tau_j/tau_cj).
Hold all non-RC voltage terms identical. With M_j=A3 |b_j|+B3 R_j Imax,
terminal-voltage defect and its partial derivatives obey

    |delta V| <= sum_j M_j |t_j|^3/6 = E_V,
    |partial_bj delta V| <= A3 |t_j|^3/6,
    |partial_Rj delta V| <= B3 Imax |t_j|^3/6,
    |partial_tj delta V| <= M_j t_j^2/2.

P2 supplies the separate free and forced derivative envelopes; linearity in b,R
and P1's Taylor bounds prove every line. Sign of terminal RC contribution is
irrelevant to absolute bounds. Other independent coordinates have zero RC-defect
partial, but the likelihood still has full cross terms with them. Any different
chart must apply the full coordinate Jacobian; dependent initial states must add
their chain derivatives. No independent-parameter covariance assumption is made.

For common |t_j|<=r, supplied voltage budget epsilon_V>0 and per-pole log-tau
slope budgets epsilon_Sj>0, sufficient conditions are

    r <= (6 epsilon_V / sum_j M_j)^(1/3),
    r <= min_j sqrt(2 epsilon_Sj / M_j).

Intersect with the declared original support; omit a zero-M constraint. For an
entire amplitude region use its suprema in M_j. These inequalities are sufficient
and conservative; they do not infer accuracy tolerance, identifiability, or
posterior containment. A query outside the derived region is uncertified by this
test, not physically impossible. Numerical evaluation also needs outward error
control before this becomes a runtime certificate. This study has no runtime gate.

## P3b: likelihood still has a horizon dependence

Fix N observations with independent Gaussian voltage likelihood and known sigma_k>0.
Let r_k=(V_k-y_k)/sigma_k and e_k=(Vhat_k-V_k)/sigma_k. Keep support and prior
identical, each observation owned once. The exact likelihood cost difference is

    Delta F = sum_k (r_k e_k + e_k^2/2).

Thus |e_k|<=eta_k=E_V,k/sigma_k gives

    |Delta F|<=sum_k (|r_k| eta_k+eta_k^2/2) = L(theta).

For a uniform admitted set with |r_k(theta)|<=rho_k and uniform eta_k, L becomes a
uniform bound L*. Then oscillation of Delta F on that set is <=2L*. With identical
prior/support and finite positive normalizers, E2 of h7_proofs_001 gives posterior
total variation <=tanh(L*/2). If applied only to a subregion this compares the two
posteriors conditioned on that same region; it does not bound omitted mass. A
realized residual bound can depend on observed y; a Gaussian model gives no finite
deterministic bound on all possible observation sequences. Non-RC errors, numerical
errors and correlated-noise whitening require their own terms or transformations.

For constant eta and rho this bound grows as N(rho eta+eta^2/2). There can be
cancellation, and parameter-independent cost shifts cancel on normalization;
the formula is a sufficient worst-case bound, not a claim every history degrades.

A concrete Gaussian counterexample shows the obstruction: two equally probable
parameter labels have exact predicted V=0 for every observed y=0, sigma=1. Approximate
predictions are 0 and epsilon!=0. The exact posterior remains (1/2,1/2); approximate
odds of the second label against the first are exp(-N epsilon^2/2), tending to zero.
Every response defect is bounded by |epsilon| independently of N. This is an
abstract likelihood example, not a claim that two specific Foster parameters have
this exact response or that Taylor produces this defect. It disproves the general
inference from bounded response defect alone to indefinite evidence fidelity.

## P3c: full joint local precision and transported uncertainty

At the SAME admitted parameter anchor, let J be the exact N-by-d whitened residual
Jacobian in one declared coordinate chart, Jhat=J+D its approximation, and Lambda
the identical positive semidefinite prior precision. Suppose P=J^T J+Lambda is
positive definite; let Phat=Jhat^T Jhat+Lambda. Set

    A=J P^(-1/2), E=D P^(-1/2), eta=||E||_2,
    epsilon=2 eta+eta^2.

Because A^T A=I-P^(-1/2)Lambda P^(-1/2)<=I, ||A||_2<=1. Expansion yields

    W=P^(-1/2)(Phat-P)P^(-1/2)=A^T E+E^T A+E^T E,
    ||W||_2<=epsilon.

If epsilon<1, then Phat is positive definite and

    (1-epsilon)P <= Phat <= (1+epsilon)P,
    P^-1/(1+epsilon) <= Phat^-1 <= P^-1/(1-epsilon).

All inequalities are Loewner matrix inequalities. They preserve every cross term;
none permits discarding off-diagonal entries. P3a's entrywise derivative bounds
can conservatively bound ||D P^-1/2||_2 by its Frobenius norm, using absolute matrix
products if only entrywise bounds are available. A well-conditioned reference or
qualified lower precision bound is substantive; small voltage error alone does not
provide it. If epsilon>=1 this certificate is inconclusive, not proof of failure.

For a scalar forecast with exact column gradient j and approximate j+d, put
q=||P^-1/2 j|| and h=||P^-1/2 d||. Then its approximate local variance satisfies

    max(0,q-h)^2/(1+epsilon)
       <= (j+d)^T Phat^-1 (j+d)
       <= (q+h)^2/(1-epsilon).

The inverse bounds and triangle/reverse-triangle inequalities prove this. If q=0,
no relative ratio is implied. The gradient must include incoming-state transport
and all physical quantities relevant to the forecast. This is same-anchor local
Gauss-Newton covariance, not the full Hessian, a nonlinear posterior theorem,
fit-to-fit agreement, uncertainty calibration, or physical coverage. H2 defects
remain outside RC-only D; RC improvement cannot certify the complete estimator.

## Decision

The fixed-degree RC representation has a bounded stationary response defect, with
explicit local value/slope budgets. It has not gained a duration-independent
likelihood or posterior guarantee. Before a deployment claim, bound the relevant
joint likelihood oscillation and transport error, or establish a different valid
evidence-preserving representation/evolution argument. Neither arbitrary evidence
decay nor support truncation follows from these proofs.
