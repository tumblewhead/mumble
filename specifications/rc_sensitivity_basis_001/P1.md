# P1: streaming RC sensitivities and compact Taylor/Hermite response

Assume the stationary reference-temperature Foster subfamily and fixed ordered
inputs/actual intervals of R1. Query coordinate t=log(tau/tau_c) has fixed public
centre and unit halfwidth, t in [-1,1]. R and incoming branch voltage are separate
amplitudes. At each fixed interpolation anchor, derivatives below are with respect
to t, not current, elapsed time or a moving fitted centre. All identities are in
exact arithmetic; rounding and finite-precision recurrence are separate checks.

## Exact derivative states

For one interval put d=dt/tau, E=exp(-d), E1=d*E and E2=(d*d-d)*E.
For free response F and forced response u, R1 gives Fnew=E*F and
unew=E*u+(1-E)*I. Differentiating once and twice yields

    F1new=E*F1+E1*F,
    F2new=E*F2+2*E1*F1+E2*F,
    u1new=E*u1+E1*(u-I),
    u2new=E*u2+2*E1*u1+E2*(u-I).

All right-hand values are pre-interval values. Initially F=1, F1=F2=0 and
u=u1=u2=0. Product-rule differentiation proves the recurrence; induction proves
it for any finite ordered stream. No observation is used twice: derivatives are
features of the same response, not additional evidence. Constant current with
zero initial forced state gives u=I*(1-F), u1=-I*F1, u2=-I*F2, preserved by these
recurrences. This is an exact arithmetic check, not a lifetime rounding bound.
Known thermal multipliers independent of the reference tau can replace I and d
by their effective values; uncertain calibration/aging requires its own model.

## Equal-width quadratic Taylor candidate

Store f(0),f'(0),f''(0) for each free/forced branch response and evaluate

    P2(t)=f(0)+f'(0)*t+f''(0)*t*t/2.

Its Chebyshev coefficients are [f(0)+f''(0)/4, f'(0), f''(0)/4]. Thus it fits the
existing three-term response-feature layout with no larger joint factor. It
matches the value and first two derivatives at the centre. For f in C^3([-1,1])
and M3=sup |f'''|, Taylor's integral remainder and its differentiated form give

    |f(t)-P2(t)| <= M3*|t|^3/6,
    |f'(t)-P2'(t)| <= M3*t*t/2.

These are conditional bounds, not assertions that M3 is small or that the
candidate improves a whole-domain fit. A local Taylor polynomial can lose value
accuracy away from its centre even while fixing the local slope.

## Four-term cubic Hermite candidate

Let a=1/sqrt(2). Store [f(-a), f(+a), f'(-a), f'(+a)]. There is a unique cubic P3
matching those four data: a homogeneous difference polynomial has two distinct
double roots and degree at most three, so is zero. Solve the fixed four-by-four
Chebyshev value/derivative collocation matrix once to form its coefficients.
The streamed derivative states are exact at anchors before finite rounding.

For f in C^4([-1,1]), generalized Rolle's theorem gives, away from the anchors,

    f(t)-P3(t)=f''''(xi)/24 * (t*t-a*a)^2

for some xi in the interval containing t and both anchors. At an anchor the value
error is zero. For symmetric anchors in [-1,1], choosing a*a=1/2 minimizes
max_(|t|<=1) (t*t-a*a)^2: this maximum equals max(a^4,(1-a*a)^2), and balancing
its two arguments gives 1/4. Therefore |f-P3|<=M4/96 with M4=sup |f''''|.
This only optimizes the stated value-remainder multiplier in that symmetric class;
it does not optimize derivative error or application performance.

Do not differentiate the unknown xi(t) in that expression to claim a slope bound.
Instead let h_q(t),g_q(t) be the four cubic cardinal functions for values and
slopes at x_q=-a,+a, so P3=sum h_q*f(x_q)+g_q*f'(x_q). The functional
L_t(f)=f'(t)-P3'(t) annihilates all cubics. Taylor's integral representation from
-1 consequently gives

    L_t(f)=integral_-1^1 K_t(s)*f''''(s) ds,
    K_t(s)=(t-s)_+^2/2
            -sum h_q'(t)*(x_q-s)_+^3/6
            -sum g_q'(t)*(x_q-s)_+^2/2.

To prove this, write f(x) as its cubic Taylor polynomial at -1 plus
integral_-1^1 (x-s)_+^3*f''''(s)/6 ds, apply L_t, and differentiate the kernels
once. Continuity permits the operations. Thus |f'-P3'|<=M4*integral |K_t(s)| ds.
The piecewise-polynomial kernel supplies a route to a certified slope envelope;
finite sampled derivatives or numerical quadrature alone do not certify its
supremum over a physical trajectory family. No such global M3/M4 is qualified here.

For the single free exponential with dose D=sum dt/tau,
f''''=(D^4-6D^3+7D^2-D)*exp(-D); differentiating R1's third derivative proves it.
Forced response is a chronological combination whose derivatives must likewise
be bounded on the admitted input/history family. One small current fixture does
not establish a universal or ten-year derivative bound.

## Joint likelihood, transport and resources

Apply either projection separately to each branch's free/forced response. Retain
the one joint QR factor over all lifted features, including cross-RC, OCV and H
terms. Derivatives alter the representation of the response; they are never
extra measurement rows. State transport uses the same interpolated responses,
and its feature derivatives obey the chain rule in the original public chart.
Keep support, prior, input order, boundary amplitudes and evidence ownership fixed.

With the unchanged six-term H2 forced/free basis, Taylor2 retains width 34,
4760 B packed factor and 312 B response state. Hermite3 and ordinary cubic RC
collocation both have width 40, 6560 B factor and 360 B response state. Adding the
existing 96 B domain/scalar allowance gives lower subtotals 5168 and 7016 B.
Tables, calibration, prior, optimizer, scratch, metadata, stack and platform memory
remain excluded. Fewer anchor exponentials do not by themselves prove lower CPU
cost; derivative arithmetic and numerical range also matter. Neither candidate
is firmware-ready, globally bounded or promoted by this proof.
