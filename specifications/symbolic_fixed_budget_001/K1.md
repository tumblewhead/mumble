# K1: one physical RC kernel must generate free and forced response

Premises: stationary tau>0, constant known reference temperature, finite ordered
nonnegative intervals, and piecewise constant current I_k. Free/forced states
start F0=1,u0=0 at the retained origin and initial branch voltage is b. R>0 is
fixed. This is the same stationary subfamily used in the studies. Known thermal
time changes need consistently transformed drive/time; no aging law is asserted.

The exact branch is v=bF+Ru, with F(t)=exp(-t/tau),
u(t)=integral_0^t [exp(-(t-s)/tau)/tau] I(s) ds. The forcing kernel is
-dF/dt, not an unrelated response family. For constant current I,

    u(t)=I(1-F(t)),
    v(t)=RI+(b-RI)F(t).

Integration proves the first identity; substitution proves the second. Derivatives
with respect to log(tau) obey partial^n u=-I partial^n F for n>=1. In particular
b=RI implies constant v=RI for every tau and time; its tau sensitivity is zero.

Let Ff be the free approximation and Fu the free function whose complementary
step response generates the forced approximation under constant current:
uhat=I(1-Fu). Define C=uhat-I(1-Ff)=I(Ff-Fu). Then

    vhat-v = (b-RI)(Ff-F) + R*C.

At b=RI this becomes RI(Ff-Fu); the corresponding log-tau slope is
RI*(partial Ff-partial Fu). A nonzero C is a violated physical identity even if
both separate approximation errors are individually small. The previous mixed
free5/forcedTaylor2 candidate has Ff=free collocation and Fu=free Taylor2 for
constant input, because P1's Taylor derivatives preserve the complement identity.
It therefore generally introduces C. Its successful all-rest case has I=0 and
cannot expose C. This does not prove C caused a particular active-fit regression.

## Piecewise-constant inputs expose the common-kernel construction

Let t_k=sum_(j<=k) dt_j, I_0=0 and Delta I_j=I_j-I_(j-1). Superposition gives

    u(t_k)=sum_(j=1..k) Delta I_j * (1-F(t_k-t_(j-1))).

One may derive this by expanding I(s) as a sum of steps and integrating each,
or by substitution into the interval recurrence. Zero-duration intervals are
allowed; multiple coincident steps telescope. Replacing F with one approximation
Fhat, with Fhat(0)=1, gives a consistent candidate step response. If
|Fhat(a)-F(a)|<=epsilon over all relevant ages, then

    |uhat(t_k)-u(t_k)| <= epsilon * sum_j |Delta I_j|.

This is a sufficient variation bound, not a duration-independent guarantee under
arbitrarily switching loads. Retaining every step would also grow memory. The
identity is architectural guidance, not a proposal to store an unbounded event list.

## A bounded-state common realization is a candidate, not a finished solution

For fixed positive rates lambda_l, define x_l'=lambda_l*(I-x_l), x_l(0)=0 and
f_l(t)=exp(-lambda_l t). Let query-dependent weights w_l(tau), independent of time
and input, satisfy sum_l w_l=1. Set

    Fhat=sum_l w_l f_l,   uhat=sum_l w_l x_l.

Constant input gives x_l=I(1-f_l), proving uhat=I(1-Fhat), including shape
derivatives where weights are differentiable. Each state evolves through the
actual interval. With sum w=1, Fhat(0)=1 and the DC gain is one. Negative weights
do not automatically preserve positivity, monotonicity or passivity; impose and
prove any such requirements separately. Finite positive rates imply internal
decay, but small kernel error, numerical stability and good inference do not
follow just from that decay. A sum of exponentials is generally not a scalar
semigroup: carry its whole auxiliary state, not merely a weighted endpoint.

A shared linear approximation operator that reproduces constants similarly
preserves constant-current complements; the existing full-family Taylor and
collocation constructions already illustrate this. Sharing the operator does
not identify free and forced stored coefficients under arbitrary input, halve
the likelihood factor, or prove a new memory saving. It removes one structural
inconsistency that a fixed-budget redesign should forbid before fitting accuracy.
